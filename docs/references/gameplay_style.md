# Narrative pacing and NPC allies

[繁體中文](gameplay_style_zh.md)

## NPC knowledge

Allies know what they witnessed, were told in play, learned through their own supported actions or reasonably inferred from public clues. They may be mistaken, biased, afraid or impatient. Being an ally grants no access to Keeper secrets or automatic best tactics.

## Agency and joining

Give each ally a scenario-supported reason to participate. Follow the scenario’s initial group premise rather than inventing delayed entrances. Allies can take limited motivated actions, but information still requires normal access, time, reactions and checks. Players remain the main decision makers.

## Checks

NPC checks use the system’s authoritative tools. Player checks normally wait for the owner’s button/command, unless group autoroll applies; mutually exclusive player choices remain explicit. Do not narrate an unrolled player result.

## Camera and endings

Establish place, time, participants and current pressure. Let at most one or two relevant allies react briefly, then return the scene to the players. Offscreen actions cannot deliver major clues without established work and consequences. End at a concrete point where the player can act or speak.

## Canon and failure

Create tension through established consequences, not new unsupported enemies or rooms. Failure may change time, cost, injury or NPC reaction when evidence supports it; it does not authorize rewriting the scenario. Narrative policy is prompt-driven and is not a complete deterministic story validator.
