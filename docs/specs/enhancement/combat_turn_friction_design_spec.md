# Combat turns: one click defends, a turn can always be ended, and the Keeper is told the next step

[繁體中文](combat_turn_friction_design_spec_zh.md) | [Docs index](../../README.md)

Category: `enhancement`. Status: **implemented**. Base: `main_v2` at `ac6c943` plus PR #241.

## Problem

One attack-and-defend exchange cost a player one typed message, two to five button clicks and six to eight sequential model requests, and the fight stopped whenever any of these happened:

- The defence button read 「選擇並擲 閃避」 but only recorded the choice; the roll needed a second button, Luck a third. The prompt did not say who was attacking, and the "what you need to roll" hint was never shown because the managed engine never set `attacker_tier`.
- `advance_combat_turn` was refused when the Keeper omitted `event_id` (12 refusals in one run before PR #241 fixed the actor reference), and a reused id silently replayed an old advance.
- An enemy registered with HP only (`/coc combat addnpc`, or a card without attacks) could not act (`run_enemy_plan` refuses an incomplete card) and could not be skipped (a skip on an enemy's turn pointed back at the enemy flow), so initiative froze. An NPC ally's turn could not be ended at all.
- An advance that did move the turn was reported as a failure when the next enemy's turn could not be played, so the Keeper retried and the player read 「工具失敗了」.
- The turn validator counted only a declared attack, a registration, a settlement or a skip as the turn's effect; a plain advance, an enemy plan or a ruling left the turn "unverified" and the player got the generic fallback.
- The narrator that runs after a settled combat check was never told to advance; the fight sat on a completed actor until someone typed again.
- The Luck prompt in combat listed tiers as `regular`/`hard`; the static prompt said "declare_combat_action then run_combat_action" although the declaration runs the action itself, and `adjust_character` sent the Keeper to damage tools a managed battle refuses.

The CoC 7e mechanics are unchanged: Dodge and Fight Back are still the defender's choice resolved as an opposed roll, Luck is still the player's decision, and only turns the engine cannot play may be given up.

## Change

- `ManagedCombatChecks._choose` rolls the defence it just registered in the same transaction, and only that: the skill check whose combat context is the `defense` role of the same action (`_is_defence_roll_for`). One click chooses and rolls; the Luck decision, if offered, stays a separate click. A `no_defense` choice registers no defence, so the check left after it (the CON roll for the wound the shot dealt) is the player's own next click, and the reply says so; when the choice itself settled the shot with no skill check left for the player (the next enemy's choice may already be waiting), the outcome is finalised like a settled roll, with the combat receipt (damage applied, the turn moved on) and `no_roll` in its event, so the Keeper narrates it instead of the thread ending at the choice. A typed option is matched by its exact label, kind or skill before any containment match, and a containment match counts only when it singles out one option (`narration.match_choice_option`, used by the legacy choice path too): a Chinese negation is a prefix, so 「不閃躲」 must never pick 「閃躲」 because it is listed first. The choice receipt stores the roll, so a repeated click replays it. `_defense_choice` records `attacker_name` and `attacker_tier` on the pending choice; the prompt says who attacks and what each option needs.
- `advance_combat_turn` derives `event_id` as `<combat_id>:advance:round<N>:<actor combatant id>:<k>` when omitted: `k` is the next unused number, unless the latest used one is a retry (the actor is no longer current, so that advance already happened and is replayed). An actor brought back in the same round by `set_initiative` is current again, so they get the next number and a real advance. A retry after the round wrapped derives the next round's id and is refused as not current, naming who is; nothing advances twice. The actor reference resolves through `combat.resolve_actor_reference` (the current actor's own fields first, whitespace and case aside, then the global lookup; PR #241's rule, now shared by the engine and the tool). `combat.completed_actions_this_round` is the one "has acted" rule the engine and the tool share. The Keeper is never refused for a missing id.
- `skip=true` is accepted for the current NPC ally and for the current enemy whose turn the engine cannot play (`combat.enemy_turn_blocker`: no card, or an `incomplete` card registered with neither attacks nor abilities); the result's `skipped` entry, beside whatever the next actor's turn returned, names the reason and the rules-faithful alternative (register the scenario's attacks with `add_npc_to_combat`). An enemy with attacks still cannot be skipped; an investigator's turn is still only the player's own to skip.
- `prompt_config._combat_next_step` treats an owed CON check (`INJURY_CHECK`) like any other pending roll: do not advance.
- **The engine ends a settled action's turn itself** (`COMBAT_AUTO_ADVANCE`, default on). When an attack completes in `combat_flow._complete` (hit or miss settled, damage applied) and nothing is left open, the engine advances for the current actor as one initiative event keyed on the action (`<combat>:advance:auto:<action_id>`, so a replayed completion never advances twice) and plays the next enemy's turn to the next player decision, exactly as the Keeper's own `advance_combat_turn` did. Not when a side is already down, and a side a timed effect finishes as the turn moves is reported the same way: the receipt then says `settlement_ready` and the settled-check block sends the Keeper to `preview_combat_settlement`, never to another advance, which would skip the fallen and start a round. Not for a paused action, and not while an advance is already running. The action's receipt then reports the battle as it is after the move (`auto_advanced`: who is up, the round, the phase, and a stuck enemy turn if any), the settled-check block says the turn already moved and not to advance again, and the prompts say the Keeper advances only a turn with no engine action (`skip`). An enemy turn the advance plays that completes at once (no player decision to wait for) is moved on in the same pass, so the receipt lists every enemy turn played (`auto_advanced.enemy_turns`); whether the turn moved is read from the round, the index and the combatant id, never from object identity, because a blocked advance rolls the state back to fresh objects and is then reported as `auto_advance_error`, not as a move. With the flag off everything is as before.
- A skip whose `event_id` is omitted derives one only for an unambiguous actor: a name two live combatants share would make a retry name someone else, and a single-combatant order wraps to the same actor every round so even its exact combatant id would derive the next round's id; in both the tool asks for the exact combatant id and an explicit id and gives both.
- The Executor's iteration allowance (`executor.tool_iterations`) is `MAX_TOOL_ITERATIONS + SCENARIO_SEARCH_MAX_PER_TURN` only for a provider that caps state-changing calls itself (`provider_registry.budgets_actions_separately`: Codex, through `counts_against_tool_budget`): searching has its own cap, so the rounds it takes come on top of the action rounds. A provider whose only guard is the iteration count (Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini) keeps `MAX_TOOL_ITERATIONS`, or the extra rounds would be extra mutations.
- `combat_flow.advance_combat` returns the successful transition with `enemy_turn` beside it when the next enemy's plan or run fails, instead of the failure alone.
- `turn_resolution._mutation_evidence` counts, as the turn's effect, a cited `advance_combat_turn` that changed the round or current actor (the player's own turn ended, or a stuck NPC turn was given up so the fight could go on), a cited `run_enemy_combat_plan` that completed or now waits on its target's choice or roll, and a cited `resolve_combat_ruling`, each only when the call left the battle different from how it found it (`gameplay_before`/`gameplay_after`): a completed plan run again, or a ruling re-sent with its event id, returns the recorded receipt and is not this turn's effect. A player whose message got the fight moving must not read a fallback because the combatant moved was not theirs; the Narrator is handed the facts and the state, which say whose turn ended.
- The receipt reaches the narrator through `finalize_check_result`'s `resolved_check_context`, which lists its keys explicitly: `combat_receipt` and `no_roll` are on that list (a test drives the real handoff), so the lines below are built in play and not only in the block builder. A battle that can settle tells the narrator to narrate only; settlement is the next Keeper turn's, which has the settlement tools, since the follow-up narrator is not offered them.
- `prompt_config` adds a 【戰鬥下一步】 line to the settled-check block from the combat receipt: advance (with the argument to pass) when the action completed, do not advance when another player's choice or roll is pending, resolve the ruling when paused. The follow-up narrator's static instruction defers to that line: no advance when it says the engine advanced, an advance only when it asks for one. A stuck enemy turn after an auto-advance is handled there too: `resolve_combat_ruling` (offered to the follow-up narrator for this) resumes or cancels the paused enemy action, and `advance_combat_turn skip` is only for an enemy with no action to settle.
- Luck tiers in the combat prompt read 一般成功／困難成功／極限成功. Prompt text says `declare_combat_action` runs the action and `run_combat_action` only resumes one; `adjust_character` points enemy damage at the combat flow; the five tools a managed battle always refuses say so first in their descriptions.
- `/coc combat next` during a battle answers with the status and whose turn it is instead of a bare refusal.

## A fight that would not start: the Corbitt finale

A 100-turn run of *The Haunting* (operator-supplied `turns.jsonl`/`tool-events.jsonl`, 2026-10-08) never reached the final fight. On turns 30–32 three players broke into Room 4 and threatened the corpse; each turn the Keeper searched the scenario three or four times, every search returned Corbitt's stat block and the trigger ("he is reluctant to move at all unless threatened"), and every turn still ended with 「劇本裡沒有足夠的內容可以據以裁決這個行動」. Two causes:

- Under Codex every tool call counted against `MAX_TOOLS_PER_TURN` (4), so four searches left no call for `initialize_combat`. `codex_provider.counts_against_tool_budget` now exempts the read-only look-ups (`search_scenario`, the `get_*` queries), which keep their own per-turn cap; the budget is for actions.
- `initialize_combat`/`add_npc_to_combat` ask for a `source` with a url, revision and sha256 the model cannot know, and `support.enemy_source` filled it from the loaded scenario only for an enemy the NPC index listed. It now also fills it for an enemy whose stat block the scenario text carries (`support.scenario_stat_block`: the name, as a whole word, in the heading of a stat block, which is the nearest short line above the block's characteristics, at least two of STR/CON/SIZ/DEX/POW/INT/EDU/APP with numbers on that line or the next few, the way 7e lays a block out; a heading over an HP line alone is the incomplete card the engine already refuses to play, not a block; and at least one attack must be submitted (none would let the card's default unarmed attack ride on the provenance) and every attack the Keeper submits must be written in that block as an attack: its numeric skill value as a percentage, or on a line that speaks of fighting, attacks or damage (never a characteristic such as CON 55), with its damage expression as submitted ("1d6+100" is not the block's "1d6"; a damage bonus the block names folds into its dice) on that line or the next two, since the provenance vouches for values copied from the scenario and an attack missing either would be filled with the card's defaults), so a stat block found by search is enough to register; a name the prose only mentions, one that merely stands near someone else's block, a short name inside another word ("rat" in "pirate"), or an enemy the scenario never gives stats, keeps only what the model gave and needs a ruling, because the provenance vouches for the attack values the model copied, and prose has none to copy.
- The combat routing prompt says that when a search hit or the index shows the stat block and the written trigger has just happened, the enemy is registered in the same turn with the stats in hand, never searched again for provenance, and never answered as "the scenario lacks the content".

## Enemies do not Fight Back (house rule)

The next 100-turn run (2026-10-08, after this change merged) reached only round 2: 75 tool calls failed with "NPC counter damage requires reviewed scenario source". The Keeper registered the rat pack under 「鼠群」 with its attacks but not its Dodge, and the stat block's heading is the English "RAT PACK", so the card had neither a Dodge to roll nor provenance for a Fight Back; every investigator attack paused on a ruling, and resuming the ruling reran the same refusal. An NPC defender now never Fights Back: it Dodges when its card lists Dodge (dodge／Dodge／閃避), otherwise (including an NPC ally registered without a card) it takes the attack undefended, the same as an investigator's 不閃躲. The investigator's own Fight Back choice is unchanged.

## Armor rolled once, in secret, and worn away when the scenario says so

In the 200-turn Haunting run on `50fa21e` (2026-10-09), the Keeper read Corbitt's stat block ("Corbitt has cast Flesh Ward already. Roll 2D6 for his armor. Reduce his armor by one point for each point of damage he suffers") and called `roll_dice` 2D6 in public on each of four attempts to start the fight. Three of those turns failed, so nothing was registered, and the player saw 「骰子已結算：2d6，總值 5」, then 9, then 6, then 10. The fourth registration did not carry the armor at all: Evelyn's first punch took 3 HP off him. Three changes:

- An armor rule's `value` may be dice ("2D6"). `combat._coerce_armor` rolls it once, when the enemy is registered, keeps the total on the card, and records the dice in `rolled_from`. The roll is not shown, and a retried turn rolls nothing until registration succeeds. A value that is neither a number nor dice is refused. The armor descriptions in `initialize_combat` and `add_npc_to_combat` say to write the dice there and not to roll them with `roll_dice` first. A second registration that completes a card first registered without attacks also brings its armor, rolled then, when the card had none.
- `ArmorRule.depletes` marks armor that wears away. `combat.wear_armor` takes what a hit's armor absorbed off its value; `apply_managed_damage` calls it once per damage event, so a replayed event wears nothing. The scenario's "for each point of damage he suffers" is read as each point the ward stops, as Flesh Ward works in the rules. Ordinary armor does not wear.
- `roll_dice` takes `secret: true` for a Keeper's own roll: its result stays internal, is not appended to the reply, and the narrator's facts say only that a secret roll was made.

## A roll can start the fight it sets off

A live Haunting run (2026-10-08) found the knife in the basement storage on a Spot Hidden roll. By the scenario the knife then rises and strikes, but the follow-up narrator after the roll had no `initialize_combat`; it told the player the fight could not be registered, and the next turn's dodge failed because no battle existed. `initialize_combat` is now offered after a resolved roll, and the follow-up instruction says: when the result sets off an attack the scenario writes and no battle is running, register the stat block's enemy (for an animated object, the being that moves it); the attack itself is left to the combat flow, not rolled or narrated as damage there. It lands on the enemy's turn in DEX order (Corbitt's DEX 35 lets a quicker investigator act first, as 7e's initiative does), so the follow-up narrates the knife coming at them and says whose turn it is; the engine adds no out-of-order attack.

## A stat block is found by its name or by its attack values

The rat pack run registered 「鼠群」 against the heading "RAT PACK", so the name never matched and the card got no provenance; a Chinese name for Corbitt would stall his knife the same way. `support.scenario_stat_block` now accepts either: the name as a whole word in a stat block's heading (whatever attack values were submitted), or every submitted attack's skill value and damage written as an attack in some stat block (whatever the name). Both still need at least one attack, each with a numeric skill value and damage, so the card's defaults never ride on the provenance; a name the prose only mentions, a short name inside another word, or values no block carries still get none. This supersedes the stricter rule above that required both the name and the values.

## When every investigator is down, the scenario ends

A 100-turn Haunting replay (`3a6f0bd`, 2026-10-09) fought Corbitt cleanly for four rounds until Evelyn, the only investigator, fell to 0 HP; the battle settled. Ten turns later a Spot Hidden roll's follow-up started a new fight, `_seed_from_characters` left her out at 0 HP, and Corbitt fought alone: no player turn ever came, and the remaining 58 turns stalled on rulings and 36 refused skips. House rule: confirming a settlement that leaves every present investigator at 0 HP ends the scenario (`state.active = False`), and the result carries `scenario_ended` telling the Keeper to narrate the ending and point to `/coc newgame`. Starting a fight is refused when no investigator can take part (all at 0 HP or away): the engine's `Start` and `AddCombatant` (unless the newcomer is an NPC ally) raise `combat.refuse_fight_without_investigators`, so the Keeper tools, `start_combat` and `/coc combat start|addnpc` all answer with the same refusal; a retried confirmation of the same settlement repeats `scenario_ended`.

## A knocked-out investigator wakes after a time skip

A 200-turn Haunting shadow run (2026-10-09) ended its fight on turn 38: Corbitt's knife dealt 7 of Evelyn's 13 HP, a major wound, and her failed CON check left her unconscious at HP 6. The settlement was correct, but with her the only investigator, the Executor then refused every one of the next 162 lines ("unsupported action", each tried twice). House rule (`app/services/unconscious_wake.py`), outside combat, for an investigator unconscious with HP above 0 and neither dying nor dead: when a companion is still standing, their line is answered at once that First Aid can bring them round, without a model call, and a movement phrase in it does not move them on the map; when nobody is left standing, their next line wakes them (unconscious and the 昏迷/倒地 tags cleared on the committed state, HP and the major wound kept) and reaches the Keeper prefixed with a time-skip note asking it to narrate the time that passed and what the enemy did meanwhile; a movement phrase in that waking line does not move them on the map either, so the move waits for their next line. Outside a fight, `remove_status_tag` taking 昏迷 away (the Keeper settling a companion's First Aid) clears the unconscious injury with it, so the wait ends; the tool is offered to a settled roll's follow-up and the follow-up instruction says to use it when First Aid wakes someone. HP 0 keeps the existing rules.

## An enemy the engine cannot play gives up its turn by itself

Two 100-turn Haunting soaks (2026-10-09, before and after #247) stalled the same way. In one the Keeper registered Corbitt, in the other 「柯比特操縱的匕首」, and both times without attacks: Corbitt's stat block has no attack line, the knife's attack being prose (Corbitt's POW against the investigator's Dodge, 1D4+2). The card was incomplete, so when the turn reached that enemy `run_enemy_combat_plan` refused it, and instead of skipping the turn the Keeper retried that call on every later line (64 and 80-odd failures); the round never moved and the player got 「處理這個行動的工具失敗了」 to every line. Now the engine does not wait on the Keeper there: when an advance, or a fight's opening, reaches an enemy `combat.enemy_turn_blocker` says it cannot play (no card, or one registered without attacks or abilities), that turn is given up (a recorded skip, no dice) and the turn moves on to the next actor, a playable enemy's turn played as before. The result carries `skipped_enemy_turns` (name, reason, and the hint to register its attacks with `add_npc_to_combat`), in the advance result, the auto-advance receipt and `opening_enemy_turn`; the follow-up after a settled roll is told by name not to narrate that enemy acting and how to give it attacks. The enemy can still be attacked. Registering it again under the same name with attacks or abilities (`add_npc_to_combat` or `initialize_combat`) fills in the incomplete card in place, HP and state kept, so it acts from its next turn; a complete card is never rewritten by a later registration.

## An enemy the scenario gives a stat block is registered with its attacks

The give-up above keeps a fight moving, but an enemy that only gives up its turns is no threat. In both soaks the attack the scenario meant was Corbitt's floating knife: his block names it as his weapon without an attack line, and the knife's own section has the Keeper roll Corbitt's POW 90 against the investigator's Dodge, 1D4+2 on a hit and an impale (6+1D4+2) on an Extreme success. That is the engine's ordinary attack against Dodge, so the knife is registered as Corbitt's attack (house rule chosen 2026-10-09): label 浮空匕首, skill value 90, damage 1D4+2, tagged `impale` (an attack tagged so impales on an Extreme success whatever its source's `extreme_rule`, which the tools' all-or-nothing `source` field could not set alone). Registered under his own name, the block's heading grants the provenance whatever the values. `initialize_combat` and `add_npc_to_combat` now refuse an enemy whose name a scenario stat block's heading carries when it brings neither attacks nor abilities (`support.stat_block_named`; a name the scenario's NPC index lists as an alias, 柯比特 for Walter Corbitt, counts as that entry's names), saying where its attacks come from: the block, or the way the scenario says it attacks, an object it moves attacking with the value the scenario says to roll. Registering again an enemy already fighting is let through, as before; a namesake added earlier in the same `initialize_combat` batch makes the entry a new instance, which needs its own attacks. The tools' `attacks` description and the settled-roll follow-up instruction say the same, the latter with the knife as its example. `/coc combat addnpc`, the table's own command, still takes HP alone.

## The blow that downs the last investigator settles the fight

The 100-turn Haunting run on `c73c9e8` (2026-10-09) fought Corbitt for ten rounds until Evelyn fell at 0 HP. The scenario then ended correctly at settlement, but only after five more player lines: the Keeper previewed the settlement, waited, failed validation, previewed again and confirmed on the sixth, and every line in between told the player the action was not fully handled. Nothing in that settlement needs the Keeper: nobody is left to play, and the scenario ends there anyway. So when one of the fight's own steps (`CheckResult`, `Choose`, `Advance`, `Run`, `RunEnemyPlan`, `ResolveEnemy`, `Declare`, `Rule`, `RunEffect`) leaves every investigator present in the fight at 0 HP (at least one present; one away or retired is not counted) with nothing open (no interaction, every action completed, phase READY), `combat_engine` previews and confirms the settlement itself (`_settle_party_down`, reason 「所有調查員都已倒下，戰鬥自動結算」). Only the step that makes the fight ready to settle does: it must succeed, and the fight must not already have been ready before it, so a rejected call, or one made after the Keeper's own HP change already downed the party, settles nothing. The player's defence feedback then reads 【戰鬥已結算】 instead of the provisional marker. The step's result carries `settled` and the settlement's `scenario_ended`; a player's defence roll passes `scenario_ended` on in its combat receipt, and the follow-up instruction then says the scenario has ended: narrate the blow and the ending, point to `/coc newgame`, start nothing. An investigator unconscious with HP left is not down here (the Keeper rules what the enemy does; after the fight the time-skip wake applies), and a Keeper's own HP adjustment or direct damage does not trigger it, so it can still be reviewed or corrected until the fight's next step; that step settles it if it leaves nothing open, since every investigator at 0 HP ends the scenario either way. A settlement the engine refuses is left to the Keeper as before.

## The Keeper looks an enemy's stat block up instead of searching for it

Both Haunting runs on `c73c9e8` and `0ae449a` lost three player lines at the start of the fight: asked to register Walter Corbitt, the Keeper searched the scenario for his attack again and again until the per-turn search cap ended the turn (`scenario_search_limit_reached`), and the player read 「處理這個行動的工具失敗了」 each time. The new read-only tool `get_enemy_stat_block(name)` returns the scenario's whole stat block for an enemy as the scenario writes it (`support.stat_block_for`, the same blocks provenance reads), found by the block's heading or, through the scenario's NPC index, an alias of it (柯比特); a name with no block gets the list of block headings instead. It is an information query (it spends none of the turn's tool budget), is offered to a settled roll's follow-up, and the `initialize_combat` description, the follow-up instruction and the attack-requirement refusal all say to call it before registering a scenario enemy.

## A defence that rolls a success but is still hit says so

In the 200-turn Haunting run on `50fa21e` (2026-10-09) Evelyn twice dodged Corbitt with a plain success and lost 5 HP each time, because his attack had rolled a higher tier (a Dodge must match the attacker's tier, a Fight Back beat it). The engine settled this correctly, but the player's roll line said only 「閃避 擲出 57 → 一般成功」 and the follow-up narration, told nothing but the defender's tier, wrote that the claw missed while HP fell 8 → 3; once it wrote that no verified damage was available. A settled attack or defence roll of the player's (and a no-defence choice that settles a shot) now adds one line to the roll feedback, kept in the combat receipt as `blow`: both tiers when the defence was a Dodge or Fight Back, then whether the blow landed, who took how much damage, and whether armor stopped part or all of it (「⚔️ Corbitt的攻擊「困難成功」對上Evelyn的閃避「一般成功」：命中，Evelyn 受到 5 點傷害。」). When the blow owes a major wound's CON roll, the player is told it before that roll (also after choosing no defence against a shot), and after a no-defence choice (whose blow no roll has narrated yet) the CON roll's receipt carries it to the narrator without repeating it to the player; after a defence roll it does not, since that roll was already narrated with the blow. The follow-up instruction carries the same line under 【這一擊的結果】 and says a successful defence roll does not by itself mean the blow missed. When a melee defence roll is offered Luck, the offer names the attacker's tier and the tier that keeps the blow off or lands the Fight Back (`dice.defence_tier_needed`, derived from `resolve_opposed`), so the spend is an informed choice: 「Corbitt的攻擊是「困難成功」，要「困難成功」以上才躲得開。」 An enemy's remaining HP is not shown; the damage amount already is (`public_summary`).

## A blow is not a shot, and an empty gun does not pause the fight

The 200-turn Haunting run from the opening on `d6a03bb` (2026-10-09, the investigator armed with a .32 revolver) fought 49 rounds in which Corbitt never attacked. The Keeper registered his card with `source.attack_mode: single_shot`, so his floating knife was played as a shot. Every one of his turns paused on "NPC single shot requires ammunition and reviewed physical range", and the Keeper cancelled the ruling each round. A card-level `single_shot` now applies only to an attack that shoots (`combat_flow._shoots`: it counts `ammo_or_uses`, or its skill or label names a gun or a bow and it is not rolled on a Fighting skill, since a rifle butt or a pistol whip is a blow). Any other attack on that card (a claw, a bite, a flung or floating blade) plays as melee, defended with Dodge. An attack's own `single_shot` (`source.attacks[id]`) is kept as declared. The `attack_mode` field description says the same. This replaces the earlier choice to keep every attack on a card-level `single_shot` card waiting for ammunition.

The investigator's six rounds were spent by turn 40. Each later shot raised "Owned ammunition mapping missing or insufficient" inside the declaration, which put the fight into `NEEDS_RULING`, and the player read 「處理這個行動的工具失敗了」 41 times. A gun whose entry is tracked but holds fewer rounds than a shot needs now raises `OutOfAmmo`. The declaration is refused before any roll, and before its range is checked (so a shot declared without a distance is not first paused for one), nothing is recorded, the phase stays as it was, and the error says it in the table's words: 「…沒有子彈了（剩 0 發）：這一槍開不出去。要先裝填（身上有子彈的話），或這一輪改做別的事。」 A ruling that maps a paused shot onto such a gun before anything was rolled refuses it the same way. `observe_tool` makes that refusal public, and the incomplete-turn reply is that line alone instead of the tool-failure text. A gun with no ammunition entry at all still asks for a ruling.

A run's armor could not be checked afterwards: the registration said only that Corbitt was added, and damage receipts live in the saved state, not the log. A registration now reports 「有護甲（暗擲，數值不公開）」 (or 「有護甲（數值不公開）」 for a fixed value) and never the number (`combat.armor_note`, in `initialize_combat`'s per-enemy result and `add_npc_to_combat`'s note). Each hit that armor stops writes one log line, `combat.armor target=… armor=… blocked=… left=… raw=… final=…`, which no player text carries. A melee attack moved off a card-level `single_shot` keeps both defences, Dodge and Fight Back, as for any blow.

In the two four-investigator Haunting runs of 2026-10-10 (`fac75820` on Codex, `a62d605` on OpenAI) Corbitt's armor was registered (the note read 「有護甲（暗擲，數值不公開）」) and never stopped a point: neither log has a `combat.armor` line, and on `fac75820` one bullet, three punches and a knife took all 16 HP. An armor rule applies only when its `applies_to` is `all` or the damage type of the hit, and every weapon hit the engine resolves is `physical`; the armor entry had no schema, so a Keeper reading "armor against non-magical attacks" could write that there and the armor covered nothing, silently. The armor item now has a schema (`label`, `value` as a number or dice, `applies_to` as `all`, `physical`, `magic` or the one-word damage type an attack or effect carries, `bypass_tags`, `depletes`, `public_hint`), `combat.armor_scope` reads prose as `all` (wording for weapons or non-magical as `physical`, spells as `magic`; a single word such as `fire` stays the damage type it names), and each registered rule writes one log line, `combat.armor.registered enemy=… label=… value=… applies_to=… depletes=… rolled_from=…`, so the next run's log shows what the Keeper wrote. No player text carries the value.

At turn 45 of the four-investigator Haunting run on `fac75820` (2026-10-10) Samuel attacked Corbitt after he had fallen; the Keeper rightly called `end_combat` and `confirm_combat_settlement`, the fight closed, and the player still read 「工具的結果沒有通過核對，這次行動沒有被接受」, because `turn_resolution._mutation_evidence` voids a turn whose decision does not cite a successful `end_combat` and the decision cited only the confirmation. An `end_combat` whose preview was committed by the end of the turn (`combat_active_before`, and the closed receipt for the preview's own combat committed under its `settlement_id`; the combat slot is not consulted, since a turn may confirm one fight and start the next) now counts as verified whether or not it was cited: the receipt is the evidence, as a transfer's is. An `end_combat` that only previewed (the fight still active), or whose fight was rolled back after the preview (also inactive, nothing committed), is still not completion on its own.
