# Separate narrative presentation from world authority

The Keeper must preserve what players heard without treating every previous narration as a source of world truth. We will retain conversation history as presentation, while projecting consequential facts only from scenario evidence whose conditions have been met, committed events, verified fact/clue records, or an explicit authorized KP decision. Player claims and unverified records remain available for harmless continuity and correction but cannot advance the plot or authorize later mechanics. This avoids a fixed per-turn fact-review model call and preserves the existing player correction path; a natural-language correction may directly repair nonconsequential presentation, while changes to resources, scenario facts, or mechanics require the corresponding authority and durable correction record.

The Keeper remains free to narrate and let players acquire incidental objects. A committed inventory change proves possession, while an object's clue status, hidden contents, or special mechanical use requires separate support. Plot relevance is not inferred from an object's name or from the Keeper once describing it as important; a player can directly correct that unsupported implication.

An item can be a scenario-backed access item without being a clue. A clue reveals information; an access item enables an action when the particular use is supported. The item's existence, possession, use, and information content are separate facts.

## Considered options

- Treat all delivered Keeper prose as canon: preserves continuity but compounds a single mistaken quantity, item, or location into future rulings.
- Discard prior prose: prevents that amplification but loses what players actually saw and heard.
- Keep presentation and authority separately: retains both histories, at the cost of provenance and projection work.
